    CCC (Claude Command Center)

    CCC (Claude Command Center) is a local dashboard for people running several coding agents at once. Instead of owning execution, it reads each engine's on-disk state as the source of truth, so a session you started in a terminal, resumed by hand or launched headless outside the dashboard still shows up on the board - that is the difference between a dashboard and a second place you have to remember to work from. On top of the board it adds a needs-attention lane that flags sessions ending on a real question, a per-session context meter, plan-usage and rate-limit projections, a Kanban with a project tree and an infinite Flow canvas, group chats between agents, a work queue with verify-to-close receipts, git worktrees per task, and an optional fallback that keeps a limit-hit session running on a $0 model. The stack is deliberately plain: a stdlib-only Python server bound to 127.0.0.1 and a no-build vanilla-JS UI, with readable JSON state under ~/.claude/command-center/. Claude Code is first-class; Codex, Cursor, Antigravity, Kilo Code, Kimi Code, OpenCode and Devin spawn and ingest with documented gaps. Version 5.39.0 shipped 2026-10-18 and the repository was pushed the same day this review was written.

    Best for: Developers who already run several coding agents and keep losing track of them in terminal tabs. CCC is worth a look if you launch Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and friends from a terminal and want one local board that shows every session, which one is waiting on you, and which has quietly run out of context - without routing your work through someone else's cloud. It is not a fit for a single-session user (a terminal is enough), for anyone who wants a hosted team product with accounts and permissions, for a Windows or Linux shop expecting parity with the macOS app, or for a buyer who needs a company entity and a governing-law clause before installing software.

    Savingy

    Savingy is an India-first expense tracker that turns what you log into one number - what is safe to spend today - plus a runway to month end, a monthly plan with budgets and goals, and a Sunday check-in it calls the Weekly Reset. Entry is manual by design: one line such as 'chai 40 upi', a receipt photo, a PDF, CSV or Excel statement import you review before anything is saved, or voice on Pro. There is no bank linking and it never asks for a banking password or PIN. The free core is not a trial - unlimited records, budgets, goals, the Weekly Reset, tracker history, reports and export cost nothing - and Pro is sold as fixed access, not a subscription: Rs 149 for 30 days or Rs 1,188 for 365 days, one payment each, with nothing renewing automatically. A one-time 45-day Pro trial opens after 30 real expense or income entries, at the user's choosing, with no card. The web app is the same product as the Android app on Google Play (com.rhnmaheshwari.savingy, updated 11 October 2026), which prints the same two prices and the same 'no subscription and no automatic renewal' line. The operator is Savingy Solutions Pvt. Ltd. (CIN U62013WB2025PTC282100), Tower House, 2A Chowringhee Square, Kolkata 700069, with Rohan Maheshwari named as director, and a Security & Trust Center that names its subprocessors - Microsoft Azure and MongoDB Atlas in Central India, Razorpay for payments, OpenAI for the assistant. The gap a buyer should know about is documentation drift rather than the product: the Privacy Policy and Terms still call the counterparty 'Savingy, Inc.', the December 2024 refund policy describes a renewing subscription the site does not sell, and the Terms still advertise Basic, Pro and Expert plans that do not exist.

    Best for: People in India who will log their own spending - by hand, one line, a receipt, a statement import or voice - and want one safe-to-spend number each day plus a forecast, without handing a banking password to an app; a sensible fit for anyone who would rather pay once for 30 or 365 days than start another auto-renewing subscription

    TinyCommand

    TinyCommand is one platform holding eleven connected products - Discovery and Enrichment, Forms, Tables, Workflows, AI Agents, an email designer, Email & campaigns, Portals, SEO and AI visibility - so a form, the record it creates, the workflow that reacts to it, the agent that researches it and the email that follows all live in the same place instead of in six subscriptions. Forms are dynamic rather than static: they can verify a field, look up a person mid-fill, branch, and take payment through Stripe, Razorpay or UPI, and forms and responses are unlimited on every plan including the free one. Tables behave like a light database with six views, 37 field types and AI columns that fill themselves; Workflows run on a visual canvas with 60+ node types, seven trigger types and human sign-off steps; AI agents use six built-in tools and can be embedded on a site or run inside a workflow; email design and broadcast campaigns reuse the same table data. Credits are the meter for automated work rather than for data collection - a workflow step costs 1 credit, an AI agent step about 10, a company lookup 20 and a person lookup 10, with no charge when a lookup misses. Vision and AI agent features run on TinyCommand's own self-hosted models, and the Privacy Policy says customer data processed by those features is not sent to third-party AI providers. The company is TinyCommand LLC of Sheridan, Wyoming, the terms are governed by Wyoming law, and the legal set includes a DPA covering the EU and UK GDPR, India's DPDP Act 2023 and CCPA/CPRA with SCC and UK IDTA transfer mechanisms. Buying runs through the vendor's own signup and checkout; the free tier needs no card.

    Best for: Teams replacing a stitched-together stack - Typeform or Jotform plus Airtable or Notion plus Zapier or Make plus Mailchimp plus a Clay-style enrichment tool plus a chatbot - with one login, one bill and one data model: forms that call apps mid-fill, tables that hold the records, workflows with 60+ node types across 458 integrations, AI agents that read and act on your own data, email campaigns sent from those tables, and login-gated client portals. It fits solo operators on the free-forever tier, growing teams that want the agent builder at $49/mo, and agencies that need many workspaces and seats at $149/mo. Not a fit if you need the single best tool in any one category (the point here is breadth, not depth), a native mobile or desktop app (the platform is browser-only), a published sub-processor list to hand to a compliance reviewer today, a longer execution history than 15 days on Free and Basic, or if the Black Friday and lifetime offers matter to you - both are real but neither is mentioned on the pricing page.
