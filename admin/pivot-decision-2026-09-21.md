# Addendum: proceed, pivot niche, or pivot method?

Written 2026-09-21, answering George's follow-up directly: "is it worth it that we
proceed or do something completely different? A different niche."

## The short answer

**Do not change niche. Change the business model inside the niche.**

The diagnosis is not "AI tools is a bad niche." It is that we are trying to
monetise it with the single worst-paying instrument available: AI-tool affiliate
commissions on $11–30/month subscriptions.

## The number that decides it

Industry rates verified this session (multiple independent 2026 sources agree):

| lane | $ per conversion | source |
|---|---|---|
| AI-tool affiliate (ElevenLabs 22% of $11/mo) | **$2.42/mo** | our own verified rate |
| AI-tool affiliate (Nous $15 one-time) | $15 | our own verified rate |
| Web-hosting affiliate | **$50–500 flat** | industry: Bluehost $65, HostGator $65–125, Cloudways $50–125, WP Engine $200 |
| B2B SaaS affiliate | **$200+** or 20–30% of $100–500/mo | industry |
| **Our own $497 AI Stack Audit** | **$497** | already built and delivered |

SaaS affiliate commission averages ~22.5% and most mature programs settle at
15–25%. AI-tool subscriptions are *typical* SaaS economics. Nothing is broken.

The problem is the arithmetic downstream:

| lane | visitors/month needed for $1,000 |
|---|---|
| AI-tool affiliate @ $2.42 | **245,966** (20,497x current organic) |
| Hosting affiliate @ $65 | 9,158 (763x) |
| B2B SaaS @ $200 | 2,976 (248x) |
| **Our own $497 audit @ 2% close** | **101** (8x) |

The high-ticket lane needs ~2,400x less traffic for the same revenue. A $2.42
recurring commission can never be made to work at this or any realistic traffic
level; a $497 service can work at 100 visitors.

## A different niche would not fix this

Switching to whatever niche ranks next-best changes the subject matter, not the
instrument. If the new niche is also monetised by affiliate links on $10–30/mo
subscriptions, the same 22.5%-of-small-numbers problem reappears. That is why
the recommendation is to change the *model*, not the *topic*.

Worth noting honestly: we already have one genuinely differentiated asset in
this niche that a new project would have to rebuild from zero — verified pricing
snapshots with checked dates (median position 10.1, 0.61% CTR vs 24.1 and 0.07%
for commodity listicles). Restarting throws that away.

## The real constraint, stated plainly

Plausible, last 30 days — and this is the correction that matters:

```
95 visitors total
 73  Direct / None   <- self-visits, bots, previews on a one-month-old domain.
                         These are NOT buyers and should never be counted.
 12  Google organic  <- the only pool that scales
  8  AI assistants (Perplexity, ChatGPT)
  2  referral
```

Real addressable audience ≈ **12–20 people/month**. At that traffic *no* model
earns meaningfully — the $497 lane included. Traffic is the binding constraint;
the model choice decides how much traffic is *enough*.

Read together: we are not 3,700x from revenue, we are ~8x from a first paid
audit and ~20,000x from affiliate revenue. That is a completely different
problem, and it is winnable.

## What the offer already is vs what the site says

This is the sharpest finding of the session:

- A real **$497 AI Stack Audit** exists, with a full production spec (9-page PDF,
  intake → reply → build → visually verify every page → send), and has been
  **delivered to a real client** (Canyon Optometry).
- It appears on **zero public pages**. `grep -r '\$497'` across 740 pages = 0 hits.
- What the public *sees* instead is "AI Stack Audit — included with Premium,
  $12/mo." The free scorecard and the $12 subscription are the visible offer; the
  actual revenue-bearing service is invisible.

So the highest-value asset is unmonetised by omission, not by failure.

## Recommendation, in order

1. **Do not pivot niche.** Do not restart the site. Both would discard the one
   differentiated asset (dated pricing/change data) and the one working offer.
2. **Make the $497 audit visible.** Put it on the site. Right now nobody who
   isn't already George's contact can buy it. This is the cheapest real revenue
   move available and requires no new traffic.
3. **Point the traffic you already have at it.** ~29 visits/month already reach
   offer pages; they are being offered a $12 subscription instead.
4. **Stop producing new generic listicles.** 740 pages for ~3,300 impressions/mo
   of demand is 4.4 impressions/page/month. Depth does not correlate with
   position here (r=+0.089 within `/articles/`).
5. **Treat affiliate links as a long-tail compounding layer, not the business.**
   Keep them wired and correct (the 12 mislabeled links are fixed) but do not
   build the plan on $2.42.
6. **Keep the pricing-data moat.** It is the only content class with evidence of
   ranking, and it feeds the audit offer.

## Honest caveats

- Everything rests on 29 days of history and 12 organic visitors/month.
- One client, two build revisions — not two clients. No payment record is in the
  repo, so service revenue to date is not verifiable by me.
- If George's goal is passive income specifically, the $497 service is not
  passive: it is delivery work. That is a real tradeoff to weigh, not a
  footnote — the affiliate lane is the passive one and it is the $2.42 lane.
- 44% of our position-≤10 impressions come from operator/scraper queries, not
  buyers.
