    Buying question

    Can you trust AI-generated citations?

    A neat footnote can be wrong. Follow the claim to the original page before letting it into a report or client deliverable.

    Quick answer

    No citation generated by an AI assistant is evidence on its own. Treat it as a lead: open the source, confirm the title and author, find the passage, and check that it actually supports the sentence you plan to publish. A real page can be cited for a claim it never made; a correct quotation can come from the wrong date or context. The check belongs to the writer, not the model.

    What a citation does and does not tell you

    A link beside a paragraph means only that someone placed a link there. It does not establish that the model read the full source, that the source is primary, or that the paragraph accurately paraphrases it. A search result snippet can omit a critical qualification. A secondary article can repeat an older figure after the original owner has revised it. Distinguish the existence of a URL from support for the exact claim.

    Research assistants such as Perplexity surface linked sources in their answers; general assistants such as ChatGPT and Claude can also propose references. The interface is a workflow choice, not a guarantee about the underlying claim. Open the original document regardless of which product produced the link.

    Check the citation in four passes

    1. Existence: open the exact URL. If it redirects, note the final page. If it fails, search for the title on the publisher's site rather than guessing at a replacement.
    2. Identity: check the author, publisher, date, and document version. A similarly titled blog post is not the report you intended to cite.
    3. Support: find the passage and compare its wording with your sentence. Does the page say “may” where the draft says “does”? Is a percentage tied to a sample or year?
    4. Current scope: check whether the claim depends on plan limits, product features, terms, or policies that may have changed since publication.

    For a numerical claim, trace it back to the original data or official report and reproduce any calculation outside the model. If the underlying evidence is inaccessible, narrow the claim to what the page you can read actually says. Do not cite a landing page as proof of a study you never opened.

    When a source is real but the answer is wrong

    Suppose a draft says a tool stores no customer data and links to its privacy page. The page might instead describe a retention period, exceptions for abuse monitoring, or different rules for enterprise and consumer accounts. The citation exists; the interpretation fails. Before making a vendor-data claim, use the data-connection questions and read the relevant policy and plan terms yourself.

    Another common mistake is borrowed authority. A product blog's description of its own feature can support “the vendor says it offers this feature,” not a claim that it works reliably for every team. For independent results, you need a documented method and actual observations. This page makes no hands-on performance claims about the assistants mentioned.

    Make verification manageable

    Build a simple claim ledger with a column for the draft sentence, source URL, exact supporting passage, checked date, and status: confirmed, narrowed, or cut. You do not need an elaborate research platform. The status column forces a decision when an attractive quote cannot be found. For longer reports, prioritize prices, security promises, legal language, numerical comparisons, and client commitments before stylistic detail.

    Ask the assistant to extract all factual claims from its own answer. This can help you see what needs checking, but never accept its “verified” label. Where sources disagree, name both and explain what each covers instead of blending them into a smooth average. Our pre-publication review pass applies the same rule to the whole document.

    What to do when the link fails

    Do not replace a broken reference with the first similar search hit. Try the publisher's own archive or current documentation; if the original cannot be found, remove the quote or attribute the narrower fact supported by a source you did open. An unavailable source is a reason to withhold the strong claim, not an invitation to reconstruct it from memory.

    For product prices and plan features, plans change: check the vendor's official page or our dated pricing research, then record the date you checked. A citation from an old plan page does not make a current price current.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does a linked answer mean the source was read?

    No. A link can come from a snippet or a source list, and the passage may not support the associated sentence. Open the page and compare the exact claim with the context around the quoted lines.

    Can I cite an AI assistant as my source?

    For a claim about what the assistant said, yes, if that is genuinely the subject. For an external fact, cite the original source you checked. The assistant's answer is not a substitute for a document or observation.

    What if two reputable sources disagree?

    Identify the dates, definitions, and populations each source covers. Present both readings if the conflict matters to the decision. If you cannot resolve the gap, state it plainly rather than selecting the tidier number.
