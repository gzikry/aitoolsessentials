    Repurposing one long asset into a week of content

    A five-step routine that turns a recording or article you already published into on-brand posts, with the claims checked and the send left to a person.

    Quick answer

    Take one long asset you already made, a webinar, a podcast episode, a recorded talk, a written guide, and turn it into a week of posts through five steps: transcribe it, choose the few passages worth pulling out by hand, draft one piece per channel, review every number and quote, then assemble a queue a person still presses send on. The AI speeds up drafting and trimming; the judgement about what is worth saying stays with you.

    Why one source beats ten prompts

    Asking a model to "write some posts about your topic" produces generic captions with no point of view. Reusing an asset you already published does the opposite: the substance exists, it is what made people engage the first time, and the work becomes extraction rather than invention. It also keeps every repurposed post traceable to something you can defend, which matters the moment a claim is questioned.

    Step one: get a clean transcript or source text

    For audio and video, start from a transcript. Descript edits media by editing its text, and Otter and Riverside produce transcripts and summaries from recordings. For a written guide, use the published text itself. Read the transcript once with a highlighter: mark the sentences that stand on their own, and strip the filler recaps before anything else looks at it.

    Step two: choose the moments by hand

    This is the step people try to automate and should not. A model will happily return twenty "key moments," most of them the middle of a thought that only works in context. Go through the transcript and pick five to eight passages that make a complete point in one or two sentences. Note the timestamp or paragraph so you can find the source again when someone asks where a post came from.

    Step three: draft one piece per channel

    Give the model the passage and the channel, not the whole transcript. A long-form post, a short video caption, and a quote card are different formats with different lengths and openings; ChatGPT and Claude both handle this when you state the format and the audience. On the visual side, Canva AI turns a quote into a branded tile, and Ideogram is the pick when the image needs legible text inside it. Ask for three short variants rather than one long one, and keep the source passage attached to each draft.

    Step four: the review pass before anything ships

    Repurposing introduces errors of its own. A trimmed quote can change a meaning; a statistic can lose the year it was true; a clip can drop the sentence that made a claim conditional. Before a post enters the queue, check every number, name, and quote against the transcript, and confirm no post states something the original hedged. A person reads the whole batch once. This is the pass that separates content which survives scrutiny from content that gets corrected in public.

    Step five: assemble the queue, keep the send human

    Notion AI or a plain document holds the calendar; Gamma turns a set of points into a carousel or a one-page summary; an automation in Make or Zapier can route approved drafts into a scheduling tool. Let it assemble and queue; do not let it publish unreviewed. A post that ships at 2am with a wrong figure costs far more than the minutes it takes to approve it.

    Where this workflow breaks

    • Thin source material. If the original was a list, the repurposed posts are a list. The workflow multiplies substance; it does not create it.
    • Over-trimming a quote. Slicing paragraphs turns "in most cases we saw" into "we saw". Keep the qualifier or do not post the line.
    • Sameness across channels. The same sentence in three formats reads as spam. Change the opening and the angle per channel, not just the length.
    • Voice drift. A model with no examples of your past posts flattens everything to its own default. Feed it real posts and edit the result.

    Frequently asked questions

    How long should a repurposed post be?

    As long as the idea needs and no longer. A single sharp sentence can carry a quote card; a two-paragraph story earns a full post. Set the length from the source passage rather than a target word count, and cut anything that only restates the original.

    Is it safe to paste a full transcript into a chatbot?

    Only under terms your organisation has accepted for that tool. A published webinar or public podcast is low risk; a recorded client call is not, because it carries names, figures, and commitments. Keep client recordings out of a personal chat plan.

    Can I automate the whole repurposing workflow?

    Automate the assembly and the queue, not the judgement. The two steps that decide quality, choosing the passages and reviewing every claim, are the two a person has to own. A pipeline that skips them produces volume that damages a brand faster than it builds one.
