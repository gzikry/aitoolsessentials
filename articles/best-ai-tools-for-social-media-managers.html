    Quick answer

    Social media managers get real return from AI in four places: platform-specific copy, on-brand visuals, repurposing long-form audio and video into clips, and keeping the calendar and the replies moving. None of them decides what you disclose about how a post was made; that stays with you and your client.

    Settle the disclosure and brand-voice rules before the tool

    Generative images and voice in a brand's feed sit closer to advertising than to editing, and the terms that matter are the client's and the platform's rather than the tool's. A workable rule: text drafting, repurposing, and scheduling can run with light review; anything synthetic a viewer could mistake for a real photo, a real person, or a real testimonial gets logged and declared wherever the client's agreement or the platform's rules require it. AI image rights and commercial use covers licensing, and reviewing AI drafts before they ship covers the review pass. Nothing here is legal advice.

    What to leave off the list for now

    • Automated engagement and reply bots. No tool here has been reviewed for it, and platforms increasingly restrict it. Treat any recommendation as a trial with account risk attached.
    • AI avatars for genuine customer stories. Fine for a product demo; presenting one as a real customer or testimonial is the kind of thing a complaint is made about.
    • Trend research without a source. Keep a cited tool such as Perplexity for what an audience is discussing, and open the source before a claim becomes a caption.

    What to buy first

    Order by the hour you most want back: a design tool if visuals are the bottleneck, the transcript editor if finishing video is, the general assistant if copy volume is. Run one paid month against real posts before any annual commitment, and read the credit model: a per-seat plan with a monthly generation cap behaves differently from a flat subscription in a launch week.     Frequently asked questions

    Can I use AI-generated images in a brand's social feed?

    Sometimes, and the deciding terms belong to the client and the platform rather than the tool. A paid plan's commercial grant is the vendor's permission to you; it does not override a contract that requires disclosure or forbids synthetic imagery in advertising.

    Will AI-written captions sound like the brand?

    Only if you give it examples of the brand's voice and edit the output. A model with no sample of past posts produces generic copy that reads like every other account, so feed it real captions and keep a person on the final pass.

    What should a social media manager pay for first?

    Whichever removes the most repetitive hour from the week, usually visual production or clipping long-form video rather than copy generation. Run one paid month on real posts before an annual plan, and check what the free tier actually caps.
