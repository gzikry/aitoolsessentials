Quick answer

People ops is a writing-heavy function with an unusually high privacy bar. AI earns its place in four places: drafting and updating policy and handbook copy, turning a process into onboarding documentation, reducing meeting notes to a shareable summary, and analysing free-text survey responses at a volume a person will not read by hand. It does not make hiring, promotion, or termination decisions. For the hiring side — sourcing, job posts, outreach — the recruiters guide covers that scope.

First, what this guide does not cover

HR information systems and applicant-tracking platforms — the Workday, BambooHR, Greenhouse, and Lever category — are not in this directory's tracked inventory, so nothing here depends on them. That matters more here than in most guides: the AI an HR team can actually buy is increasingly inside the HRIS, under a data-processing agreement scoped to employee records. If yours already includes that, it is usually the safer place to do the work.

What follows is the layer around the HR system: drafting, summarising, and organising text a person then reviews.

Where the four jobs rank

  1. Policy and handbook drafting — highest return, safest start. The source material is already non-identifiable, the output goes through human approval by default, and a first draft saves real time.
  2. Onboarding and process documentation — high return. Turning a process that lives in one person's head into a written guide is what AI drafting is genuinely good at, and the process owner reviews it.
  3. Survey and free-text analysis — moderate, overconfidence risk. Useful for grouping thousands of comments into themes; unreliable at the level of a single comment. Report themes, quote verbatim, and never present a categorisation as a measurement.
  4. Interview capture — modest. Real value on process calls, real legal exposure on interviews.

The privacy rules that decide most of these purchases

People ops handles the categories these tools are least suited to: health information, performance and disciplinary records, compensation, grievance and investigation material, and identity documents. Check the data-use and training terms for the tier you are on — a personal subscription is not an enterprise agreement. Our guides on whether AI tools are safe for business data and what happens to your data when you delete an account cover the questions, and writing a company AI usage policy is the document to write first.

Three rules worth writing into that policy: no identifiable employee data in a general assistant; no recorded interview without a stated reason and a consent step matching your jurisdiction; and no AI output presented as a decision rather than a draft.

Before you pay for anything

Check what is already included first. Frequently asked questions

Can AI tools make hiring decisions?

No, and they should not be pointed at one. Employment decisions carry legal exposure that varies by jurisdiction, and a model cannot be accountable for them. Use these tools for drafting, summarising, and structuring information a person then decides on.

Is employee data safe in a general assistant?

Only on the right tier, and often not at all. Performance notes, health information, compensation, and anything from a grievance or investigation belong in your HR system, not a chat box. Check training and retention terms for the specific plan before anything identifiable goes in.

Do I need a separate AI tool for HR, or can I use what we have?

Start with what is already paid for. Many organisations have an assistant bundled into a productivity suite and an AI feature inside their chat or document tool. Prove the drafting job there before buying another seat.
