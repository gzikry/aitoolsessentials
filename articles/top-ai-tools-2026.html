    How to choose AI tools without getting buried in hype.

    Most AI tools look impressive in demos. The better question is whether a tool reliably improves a workflow you repeat often enough to justify cost, review, and behavior change.

    Use the three-part test.

    • Job fit: define the exact work product, input, output, owner, and acceptance criteria.
    • Output quality: run three real examples and measure accuracy, edit distance, tone, and failure modes.
    • Operational cost: include setup friction, privacy constraints, collaboration support, and monthly spend.

    Start narrow.

    Pick one repetitive workflow. Document the before-and-after process. Adopt the tool only when it saves time after review, not just during generation.

    Build a stack, not a pile.

    The strongest AI systems connect tools into a workflow: capture inputs, generate a draft, verify sources, route review, and archive the final output. A small stack with clear operating rules beats a crowded stack of unused subscriptions.
