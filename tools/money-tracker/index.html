    Overview

    Money Tracker is a mobile expense tracker and budget app for Android and iPhone, published under the Google Play developer account Horoscope365 - the controller is a natural person, Zheng Kui, with a Beijing postal address on the Play developer block. You record income, expenses and transfers by hand, by voice ("lunch 12"), by photographing a receipt or bill, or on Android with Premium by confirming a transaction captured from banking or payment app notifications; Premium adds bill import, unlimited AI entry, app lock and extra books. Budgets can be set monthly and per category, with charts, category reports, multiple currencies, separate books, AA split ledgers and shared team books, and data exports to CSV, Excel or PDF. It deliberately does not link to a bank and never asks for bank credentials: without sign-in records stay on the phone, and signing in backs them up to the vendor's own server. Distribution is established rather than nominal - 5M+ Google Play installs at 4.8 stars from roughly 324K reviews, an App Store listing at 4.8 from 414 ratings, both updated in October 2026. The weakness is documentation, not the product: the marketing site publishes no price, links no legal page and prints no contact address, and the only privacy notice sits at an unlinked path that never mentions the financial data, receipt photos, voice clips or notification text the app handles.

    Key features

    • Manual income, expense and transfer entry with amount, date, category, note and account
    • AI entry by voice ("lunch 12") and by receipt or bill photo, with review and edit before saving
    • Monthly total budget and per-category limits, with overspend reminders and a configurable budget-month start on Premium
    • Charts, category breakdowns and monthly reports covering income, expenses and remaining budget
    • Multiple currencies with exchange rates, and separate books for personal, business or project spending
    • Shared ledgers: AA split books (equal, by amount, shares or percentage, with who-owes-whom), team books and two-person data sharing
    • Import from CSV, Excel, PDF or statement images and export to CSV, Excel or PDF (import is Premium)
    • Android Smart Notification Entry (Premium) that proposes a transaction from banking or payment app notifications and saves only after confirmation, with per-app blocking
    • Optional passcode or fingerprint/Face ID app lock (Premium); no bank linking and no bank credentials
    • Cloud backup and sync when signed in (Google, Facebook or Apple), local-only storage when not

    Frequently asked questions

    How much does Money Tracker cost?

    The app is free to download and its everyday features - manual entry, categories, budgets, charts, reports, multi-currency, split books and exports - work with ads at no cost. Premium removes the ads and adds unlimited AI entry, bill import, Android notification entry, app lock, more books and team books. Premium is sold only by the app stores, and the vendor's own site prints no price: the App Store lists Yearly Premium at $29.99 and Monthly Premium at $2.99, and Google Play shows an in-app purchase range of $2.99-$29.00 per item. Buy in the store whose price you can see, because nothing on the vendor's site quotes one.

    Does it connect to my bank?

    No. Money Tracker does not link to a bank and states on its site and store listings that it never asks for a bank username or password. On Android, Premium adds Smart Notification Entry, which reads transaction notifications from banking or payment apps, proposes an entry, and saves it only after you confirm - and individual apps can be blocked from being read. Everything else is manual, voice, photo or statement import.

    Where is its privacy policy, and does it cover my financial data?

    There is exactly one notice, at money.quhou123.com/Info/privacyPolicy, which is the URL both store listings cite - the marketing site itself links no legal page. Read it before installing, because it does not cover the app's core data: it lists email, name, phone, gender, passcode, usage data and device identifiers, names its ad and analytics SDKs, and never mentions transaction amounts, balances, receipt photos, voice clips or the notification text Android entry reads. Google Play's Data safety declares that Financial info is collected, so the two surfaces disagree.

    Is there a Terms of Service or a refund policy?

    Neither is published. /terms, /en/terms, /Info/terms, /terms-of-service, /eula and /Info/agreement all return 404 on the vendor's domain, even though the privacy notice refers readers to a Terms and Conditions document. Subscriptions are billed and refunded through Apple and Google, so their store policies govern the transaction - but the app-level contract a reader would normally check does not exist.

    Who runs it, and is it the same product on both stores?

    Yes. The Play listing (com.freeman.moneymanager) and the App Store listing (id6444122615) are the same app, and the same natural person, Zheng Kui, is the Play developer and the App Store seller; the Play developer block also prints a Beijing postal address and the support address freemanzk@163.com. The publisher account is named Horoscope365, which is also the operator's horoscope site at www.quhou123.com, and the App Store description body still calls the app "Money Manager" - both are brand-legibility issues on a finance app rather than signs of two products.
