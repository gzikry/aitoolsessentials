    FicWeave review

    FicWeave is a single-purpose writing workspace for fanfiction. You give it a fandom or an original universe, the characters, a relationship or trope and a one-line what-if, and it writes a first chapter you can read without an account; saving the story creates a private library and a continuation workspace. Each continuation is assembled from selected Story Memory - Characters, Relationships, Important Facts, Open Threads and a bounded summary, with every AI-created item labelled by the chapter it came from - plus recent chapters (one on Free, up to six on paid plans). The claim being sold is workflow, not prose: the FAQ says outright that FicWeave 'does not promise better prose than ChatGPT or perfect continuity', and its own case-study page discloses an earlier failed extraction and a remaining continuity error rather than presenting a clean benchmark. All three paid plans run 'the same Pro writing model capability', so the ladder buys credits and editable-story slots rather than a better model. It is 18+, the Content Policy bans explicit sexual content and any sexualisation of minors or identifiable real people, and the Terms state that FicWeave is not affiliated with or endorsed by any fandom, franchise or author a user writes with.

    Overview

    FicWeave is a single-purpose writing workspace for fanfiction. You give it a fandom or an original universe, the characters, a relationship or trope and a one-line what-if, and it writes a first chapter you can read without an account; saving the story creates a private library and a continuation workspace. Each continuation is assembled from selected Story Memory - Characters, Relationships, Important Facts, Open Threads and a bounded summary, with every AI-created item labelled by the chapter it came from - plus recent chapters (one on Free, up to six on paid plans). The claim being sold is workflow, not prose: the FAQ says outright that FicWeave 'does not promise better prose than ChatGPT or perfect continuity', and its own case-study page discloses an earlier failed extraction and a remaining continuity error rather than presenting a clean benchmark. All three paid plans run 'the same Pro writing model capability', so the ladder buys credits and editable-story slots rather than a better model. It is 18+, the Content Policy bans explicit sexual content and any sexualisation of minors or identifiable real people, and the Terms state that FicWeave is not affiliated with or endorsed by any fandom, franchise or author a user writes with.

    Key features

    • An anonymous first chapter: fandom, characters, relationship, trope and a what-if in, a readable chapter out, no account needed
    • A private story library with chapter navigation and a continuation workspace rather than one growing chat thread
    • Story Memory over five fields - Characters, Relationships, Important Facts, Open Threads and a bounded summary - with every AI-created item labelled by its source chapter
    • Editable memory on paid plans, with user edits protected from later AI updates, plus Mark resolved / Mark dropped on open threads
    • Bounded context assembly: selected memory, a summary and recent chapters (one on Free, six on paid), explicitly not the whole manuscript
    • English and Chinese mirrors of every public page under /zh, prices identical to the cent
    • Email/password and Google sign-in, with email verification and password reset enabled

    Use cases

    • Drafting a multi-chapter fanfic where chapter twelve still has to remember a detail from chapter two
    • Exploring what-if branches inside a fandom you already write in, without re-pasting canon and context every chapter
    • Original long-form fiction that needs a tracked cast, established facts and unresolved threads instead of one long prompt
    • Testing whether a paid AI writing subscription earns its price by spending the one-time free allowance first
    • Correcting a story's continuity directly - paid plans let you edit a remembered character fact before the next chapter

    Pros

    • Every processor is named with its purpose rather than described by category: Cloudflare for delivery, hosting and security, Neon for the hosted PostgreSQL database, OpenRouter and the model providers it routes to for generation, Waffo Pancake for checkout, tax, fraud checks and prompt screening, Google for sign-in and Analytics, and Resend for transactional email
    • The privacy policy states plainly that private story text is not used to train a FicWeave model and that personal information is not sold, and it separates its own practice from what third-party model providers may do instead of calling the whole chain private
    • Credit mechanics are unusually honest: 1 credit per saved continuation, nothing charged for a failed or cancelled attempt (the reservation returns to its original source and expiry), nothing charged for Story Memory updates, and saved stories and chapters stay readable when credits run out
    • It publishes its own limits instead of overselling them - /llms.txt says the Story Memory case study is 'not a published generation-quality benchmark', records an earlier truncation and a failed extraction, and admits a continuity error that remained; the generator FAQ says the sample chapter is an authored illustration, not an AI generation
    • The free allowance is described exactly rather than advertised as a free plan: first chapter with no account, then 3 non-expiring starter credits, 1 editable story, read-only memory, and the context difference stated as 'Free uses up to 1 recent chapter; paid plans use up to 6'
    • The Content Policy is explicit in both directions - what is allowed (romance, dark themes, fictional violence, non-explicit mature themes) and what is refused (explicit sexual content, sexualisation of minors, non-consensual explicit content, sexualisation of real people, moderation evasion) - with an 18+ requirement in the Terms
    • Email verification and password reset are both enabled in /api/config/public, so a mistyped address or a forgotten password has a recovery path - several listings in this catalogue ship with one or both switched off
    • Pricing reconciles across every surface that states it: /pricing, /zh/pricing, /terms and /refund-policy agree on all six figures, and the annual column is exactly ten monthly payments

    Cons

    • No registered company, trading name, postal address or named data controller appears on any page: the Terms define the counterparty only as 'the independent operator of the Service' and the privacy policy as 'the independent operator of FicWeave', and there is no governing-law, jurisdiction, venue or arbitration clause - the Terms end at 'Changes and contact'
    • Google Analytics 4 (G-YPKFQG4WCL) loads on first paint on every page, including /privacy itself, with no consent banner anywhere on the site, while the policy says processing rests on consent 'where consent is required' - a reader in the EU or UK is measured before making any choice
    • The Cloudflare Web Analytics beacon (static.cloudflareinsights.com/beacon.min.js) also ships sitewide, and the policy names Cloudflare for delivery, hosting and security but never for analytics
    • Self-service export and permanent account deletion are described in the policy as things that are 'not yet complete features', so the only route to your own data is an email to support
    • Subscription credits expire at the end of each monthly window with no rollover, including on annual plans where the full year is paid up front - three quiet months on an annual plan still burns three allowances
    • All three paid plans run the same writing model, so $24.99/month buys 400 credits and 100 editable stories rather than better output; the site says this, but a capacity ladder priced like a quality ladder is easy to misread
    • Fanfiction output depends on rights the writer holds: the Terms put responsibility for third-party characters and settings on the user and disclaim any affiliation with or endorsement by the fandoms and authors involved
    • Retention is phrased rather than periodised - story data lasts 'while your account and saved content remain active', diagnostics and logs 'only as reasonably needed', backups 'a limited period' - and no number is given for any of them

    Frequently asked questions

    What does FicWeave cost?

    Subscriptions only, in USD: Pro $7.99/month for 100 credits per subscription month and up to 20 editable stories, Pro+ $14.99/month for 200 credits and 50 stories, Max $24.99/month for 400 credits and 100 stories. Annual billing is $79.90, $149.90 and $249.90, charged once with credits granted monthly - exactly ten monthly payments, the 'Save 16.7%' the cards advertise. A saved standard continuation costs 1 credit. One-time credit packs are no longer sold, and subscription credits expire at each monthly window without rollover.

    Is there a free trial?

    No trial and no card-free paid tier. What exists is a one-time free allowance: the first chapter can be generated and read with no account at all, and after sign-in Free includes 3 starter credits that do not expire, 1 editable story, read-only Story Memory and up to one recent chapter of context. A card is only involved when you buy a paid plan.

    Can I keep my stories and chapters if I stop paying?

    Yes. Saved stories, chapters and Story Memory stay readable, and stories outside your plan's editable set become read-only rather than deleted - the pricing page and Terms both say the content is not removed. New chapters and edits need credits and an editable slot.

    Does FicWeave train on my stories?

    The privacy policy says private story text is not used to train a FicWeave model and that personal information is not sold. Story input and context are sent to OpenRouter, which routes to the selected model provider, and the policy states that those providers' retention and data-use rules differ by model and endpoint - so the vendor's promise covers its own model, not the whole chain.

    Does it write explicit content?

    No. The Content Policy allows romance, dark themes, fictional violence and non-explicit mature themes, and refuses explicit sexual content, any sexualisation of minors, non-consensual explicit content, sexualisation of identifiable real people and instructions for evading moderation. The Terms require users to be 18 or older.

    How is this different from writing fanfiction in a chat window?

    A saved library, chapter navigation, a continuation workspace and a source-linked memory panel you can read and correct - versus re-supplying context in every prompt. The vendor frames this as a workflow difference and explicitly does not claim better prose than ChatGPT or perfect continuity; continuation uses selected memory, a summary and recent chapters rather than the whole manuscript.
