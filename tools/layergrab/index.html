    LayerGrab review

    LayerGrab splits a flat image into editable, named layers with AI: upload or right-click an image, get each element (text, products, graphics) on its own transparent layer with the background filled in behind them, and export PNG, ZIP or a layered PSD. It works in the browser, a Chrome extension (listed on the Chrome Web Store), and - per its privacy page - Photoshop, Figma and Sketch plugins. The credit math is spelled out with worked examples on the pricing page (a 10-layer poster at 10 credits per layer on Basic costs $1.10). The gaps are identity and consumer paperwork: no company or jurisdiction is named anywhere, there is no refund policy page and no contact page, and the AI model doing the splitting is described only as 'our processing partner'.

    Frequently asked questions

    What does a LayerGrab split cost?

    Each split costs 10 credits on the Basic plan ($12/month for 1,200 credits) and 8 credits on Premium ($36/month for 3,600 credits) - about $0.10 and $0.08 per credit respectively. One-time packs run $20 for 1,800 credits and $60 for 6,600.

    Does LayerGrab keep or train on my images?

    Its privacy page says images are sent to 'an AI model operated by our processing partner' to be split, and that uploads are not sold and not used to train its own models. The partner is not named and no retention period is stated, so sensitive client work deserves caution.

    Is there a free option?

    The homepage advertises freemium access (free credits to try the splitter); paid plans start at $12/month and one-time packs at $20.
