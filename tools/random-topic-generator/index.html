    Random Topic Generator review

    Random Topic Generator is a speaking-practice site with a free half and a paid one.     Overview

    Random Topic Generator is a speaking-practice site with a free half and a paid one. The free half does what the name says and needs no account: it draws 1, 3 or 5 topics from a library of 250 prompts in eight languages, filtered by everyday, work, creative or opinion, and pairs each pick with a one-, two-, three- or five-minute timer while the browser records you.     Key features

    • Free draw of 1, 3 or 5 random topics from a 250-topic library, filterable by everyday, work, creative or opinion, with no account
    • Eight topic-library languages - English, Chinese, French, Indonesian, Arabic, Turkish, Thai, Hindi - independent of the site's own interface language
    • Built-in timer at 1, 2, 3 or 5 minutes with a copy-topic shortcut, so the draw, the pick and the clock are one screen
    • Custom AI topics after Google sign-in: describe the audience or difficulty and get 3, 5 or 10 generated topics, unlimited
    • AI speech report from a recording: transcript, native rewrite, grammar corrections with original-vs-corrected wording and an explanation for each
    • Pronunciation and clarity notes at word level, filler-word counts (um, uh, like, you know, and the equivalents in other languages), pacing, pauses and tone
    • Six-dimension score with published weights: grammar 20%, coherence 20%, vocabulary 15%, fluency 20%, filler words 10%, confidence
    • Practice history with cloud storage of recordings once signed in, playback and download, plus per-session delete and whole-account delete
    • Two extra free generators on the same account - YouTube tags and job titles, both login-gated to limit abuse

    Who Random Topic Generator is for

    Random Topic Generator is aimed at teams and individuals who need people practising impromptu speaking on their own terms - language learners, teachers running a class warm-up, toastmasters-style clubs, and anyone who wants to talk for one minute without freezing. the free path needs no account: draw 1, 3 or 5 topics from a 250-topic library in eight languages, pick a 1/2/3/5-minute timer, and record locally. the paid path is only for the ai speech report, which costs 100 credits a run (about $1 on every pack and plan), so it fits someone who wants grammar, filler-word and pacing feedback and is willing to pay per analysis. it is not a fit if you need a named company, a governing-law clause or a published refund term before you subscribe, if you object to ad-supported pages on a paid product, or if you want a human coach - the report is model output scored on six fixed dimensions. It is designed for education workflows where output quality, adoption effort, and operating cost all matter. Compare alternatives first if you require self-hosting, unusually deep customization, or tighter policy controls than the official plan provides.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Random Topic Generator free?

    Yes, for the part most people use. Drawing 1, 3 or 5 topics from the 250-topic library, the categories, the 1/2/3/5-minute timer and local recording are free, unlimited and need no account. What costs money is the AI speech report - 100 credits a run, about $1 - which needs a Google sign-in plus either a subscription ($19.9 a month for 2,000 credits, $59.9 for 6,000) or a one-time pack ($5 for 500 credits, $10 for 1,000, $20 for 2,000).

    Do I need to create an account to practise speaking?

    Not for the free loop. The site draws topics and runs the timer without an account, and the recording stays on your device. A Google account is needed for three things: custom AI-generated topics, saving recordings to practice history in the cloud, and the AI report. Note that the Privacy Policy and Terms currently say the opposite - that the service requires sign-in and offers no anonymous access - which we read as out-of-date legal copy rather than the shipped behaviour, and have asked the vendor about.

    What does the AI speech report actually score?

    Six weighted dimensions: grammar 20% (tense, agreement, articles, word choice), coherence 20% (thesis, structure, transitions), vocabulary 15% (diversity and register), fluency 20% (pacing and mid-sentence hesitation), filler words 10% (every um, uh, like and you know, with roughly five points off per filler per minute) and confidence. Alongside the score you get the transcript, a native rewrite, per-error grammar notes, word-level pronunciation tips, and pacing, pause and tone commentary.

    Is my recording used to train models?

    The policy commits that the operator does not use your recordings for anything other than delivering the analysis, and that recordings sit in private storage tied to your account. What it does not settle is provider-side use: processing is sent to OpenRouter and its underlying providers under their own terms, and there is no separate statement on training for that leg. You can delete a session, or the whole account, from inside the product.
