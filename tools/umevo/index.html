    Overview

    UMEVO sells three small magnetic voice recorders - Note ($79), Note Plus ($109) and Note ONE ($159) - that capture in-person meetings and phone calls and hand the audio to a phone app that transcribes it, labels speakers, summarises, translates and builds mind maps. It is the opposite trade to Otter, Fireflies or Fathom: those send a bot into a video call and are free or cheap to start, while UMEVO sells you a device and nothing joins the call, so the other party sees a recorder on the table rather than a participant in the attendee list. Note Plus transcribes live inside the companion app, which is a third-party product called AI DVR Link; Note ONE transfers over Wi-Fi and generates the notes after upload. Year one is the cheap part of the AI cost on Note Plus, because the Max plan is included; from year two the free tier drops to 400 minutes a month and the paid ladder that applies to you is the thing to check before buying, since the two product pages currently quote different prices for it.

    Key features

    • Three models: Note $79 (64GB, 5 languages of real-time translation in 59 languages via GPT-4o), Note Plus $109 (64GB, real-time transcription, NOTE and CALL modes for in-person and phone-call capture, pairs with the AI DVR Link app), Note ONE $159 (64GB, Wi-Fi file transfer, up to 40 hours of continuous recording, notes generated after upload)
    • Magnetic form factor with a magnetic ring and case so it clips behind a phone or to clothing
    • Two recording modes on the Plus and ONE - an in-person mode and a call mode that captures both sides of a phone conversation
    • Companion app does transcription, speaker identification, summaries, mind maps, text translation, template-based notes and an 'Ask AI' query over your own recordings
    • Exports to TXT, DOCX and PDF; web app for editing from a browser
    • AI plan ladder by model: Note Plus includes the Max plan for year one then falls back to 400 free minutes a month; Note ONE gives 30,000 minutes a month for 3 months then 400; the Note has no included plan
    • Accessories sold separately: magnetic carrying case $20, official 3-in-1 kit $20
    • Ships worldwide free, 30-day no-reason return, 12-month replacement warranty

    Frequently asked questions

    What does UMEVO cost, and what does the AI cost after year one?

    The hardware is a one-time purchase: Note $79, Note Plus $109 against a $169 list price, Note ONE $159 against $179, plus $20 for a case or accessory kit and a $5 shipping line. AI minutes are separate after the included period. Note Plus includes the Max plan free for year one, then drops to the free Starter plan at 400 transcription minutes a month; Note ONE gives 30,000 minutes a month for three months, then the same 400 free minutes. Paid tiers and minute packs are published on both product pages, but the two pages quote different figures for them - $7.99/$19.99 a month on Note ONE against 'Pro $2.50/mo or Max $5.83/mo billed annually' on Note Plus - so confirm which applies to the model you are buying before checkout.

    Where do my recordings actually go?

    Into the companion phone app, and the app for Note Plus is a third party: AI DVR Link, developed by Dongguan Kinghal Electronic Technology Co., Ltd., not by UMEVO. UMEVO's footer privacy notice says so in section 10, and that app's own policy states data is stored in the United States and retained about one month after you cancel your account. Note ONE generates notes after the recording is transferred over Wi-Fi rather than live. Two things are not on umevo.ai at all: a stated retention period for audio and transcripts on UMEVO's own side, and any statement about model training - the vendor told us in writing that recordings are not used to train models, but no published page says it.

    Who is the seller, and which law applies?

    The site gives three answers, and we are recording the contradiction rather than resolving it in the vendor's favour. The footer Terms name UME Technology Inc.; the store policy page for terms names Huasengdi Technology Co., Ltd. of Sheridan, Wyoming as the administrator and the entity accountable for data under the EULA; and both governing-law clauses say 'the laws of the United States' without naming a state. UME Technology Inc. is a real Colorado company (registration 20251272831, in good standing) and the vendor has confirmed in writing that it is the seller and operator, that 1312 17th St, Unit 2955, Denver is the current address and that Colorado law governs. Until that reaches the pages, treat the contracting party as UME Technology Inc. on the vendor's written word, not on the terms you are asked to accept.

    Can it record phone calls, and is that legal where I live?

    The Plus and ONE have a dedicated call mode that captures both sides of a phone conversation, which is the job meeting bots cannot do at all. Legality is on you: call-recording rules differ by state and country, several US states require every party to consent, and some countries require it of all parties regardless of where you are calling from. The product pages and the terms carry no consent warning at all - the only mention on the site is a blog post about one-party and all-party consent laws - so check your jurisdiction before recording anyone.

    How does this compare with Otter, Fireflies or Fathom?

    Those are meeting bots: they join your video call as a participant, produce a transcript, and are free or inexpensive to start. UMEVO is a physical recorder that captures rooms and phone calls, costs $79-$159 up front, and puts nothing in the attendee list. If your meetings are on Zoom or Teams with a laptop open, a meeting bot is cheaper and easier. If the conversations that matter happen in a room, on a phone call, or somewhere a laptop would be intrusive, a recorder is the only one of the two that can do the job.
