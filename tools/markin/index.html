    Overview

    Markin is an agentic growth platform for large direct-to-consumer businesses. You connect a store, CRM and warehouse; its agents read customer behaviour, propose revenue opportunities with a size attached, prepare or launch the intervention in the tools you already run, and read each result against a control group. The pitch is deliberately narrow - incremental ARPU, not more dashboards - and the vendor is unusually candid on its own comparison page that an LLM wired to your data through MCP can do parts of this work; what it sells is the assembled system, the experiment bookkeeping, the audit trail and the team that stands behind it. Access is sales-led: the free allowance and every paid plan begin with a request form, and the pricing FAQ says outright that there is no online checkout yet.

    Key features

    • Agents that read customer, transaction and lifecycle data to write their own growth hypotheses and size each opportunity before anything runs
    • Experiment design with a holdout or control group, then execution inside the customer's existing tools - CRM, messaging, ads, product surfaces - or a handoff with an owner when a human has to act
    • Connectors across the stack the vendor publishes: Shopify, WooCommerce, Adobe Commerce, PrestaShop, Klaviyo, Braze, Salesforce, HubSpot, Mailchimp, Segment, mParticle, Snowflake, BigQuery, Databricks, Stripe, Zendesk, Intercom, Slack and Teams
    • Slack and Teams delivery, so recommendations and alerts arrive where the team already works
    • An audit trail per automated decision, tenant-isolated environments, least-privilege access and time-bound support access, all documented on /security
    • Shared team credits with no per-seat fee, and a stated credit cost range per task class
    • A DPA, acceptable-use policy and published subprocessor list that a procurement team can review before a pilot

    Use cases

    • Finding revenue opportunities in an existing customer base - churn signals, second-purchase gaps, win-back timing, pricing and packaging friction - instead of waiting for a hypothesis to come from a roadmap
    • Running more experiments in parallel than a small data-science team can staff, each with a holdout
    • Turning a CRM audit into a sized list of opportunities with owners
    • Letting lifecycle teams ship 1:1 next-best-action decisions through tools they already pay for
    • Giving procurement a vendor with a named legal entity, a DPA and a published subprocessor list before any customer data moves

    Pros

    • The operator is named inside the documents rather than only in a footer: the Terms and Privacy Policy identify Celerity Software, Sociedad Limitada, NIF B70839865, Carrer Eduard Maria Balcells 49, 08173 Barcelona, with Spanish law and the courts of Barcelona for disputes - and that registration checks out independently (Registro Mercantil de Barcelona, incorporated 2024-03-07, CNAE 6201 software programming, same NIF and address)
    • A complete, dated legal set under /legal - Terms, Privacy Policy, Cookie Policy, Data Processing Agreement, Acceptable Use Policy and a Subprocessors page - all 'Last updated: 26 October 2026'
    • The subprocessor list is the specific kind a security review can act on: AWS (eu-west-1), Google Cloud (europe-west1), Cloudflare, Snowflake, OpenAI and Anthropic each marked opt-in with 'no training' and their transfer basis (EU/US SCCs) stated, Datadog, Sentry, HubSpot and Google Workspace - every one with a purpose and a region
    • Retention is periodised rather than phrased: account data for the life of the customer relationship, marketing contacts deleted or anonymised on opt-out, log and usage data up to 24 months, service data governed by the DPA
    • The vendor states in its own pricing FAQ that 'there is no online checkout yet' and that nothing is charged until its team confirms access, configuration and billing - the opposite of a storefront that looks open and is not
    • Credit mechanics are written down instead of implied: rollover for one extra period with oldest-first consumption, capacity reductions at renewal, credits still granted monthly on an annual plan, and new credit-consuming work paused rather than auto-charged when the balance runs out
    • The comparison page gives the honest version of the pitch - asked whether an LLM through MCP can do this work it answers 'Yes. With the right tools, context and workflows, a general-purpose agent can analyse data and take action. Markin packages ongoing growth work into a dedicated system'
    • Measurement language is disciplined throughout - 'a reported conversion is not automatically an incremental gain', control groups and holdouts in every product description - and the security page documents autonomy limits rather than only claiming AI governance
    • [personal address withheld], the submitted address, is a real single mailbox: an SMTP RCPT probe against the domain's Google Workspace MX returns 250 for roma@ and 550 for a random local part, so the domain is neither a catch-all nor a dead address

    Cons

    • There is no self-serve path at all: the free card's button reads 'Request free access', the Pro card's reads 'Request Pro', and 'Get started for free' in the navigation lands on the same /apply form - no signup, no card entry, no workspace to look at before a sales call, so a reader cannot evaluate the product today the way they can a $9 tool
    • The homepage leads with 'Markin performs 38% better than an LLM connected through MCP' and bars for Claude Opus 5.5, Kimi K3, Qwen3.8, GLM-5.3 and GPT-6 Astra, but the 'See the benchmark methodology' link opens /compare/markin-vs-llm-mcp, a positioning essay that publishes no task set, prompt, scoring rule, run count or date - the number cannot be checked from anything the site serves
    • '4.9/5 on G2' is displayed on /pricing, /apply and /book-a-demo, while G2's own indexed seller pages for the Markin name read '4.0 stars by 2 verified reviews' and, on a second seller profile, '5.0 stars by 1 verified review'; G2 returned 403 to our fetch, so the badge could not be reconciled with the public listing either way
    • ISO 42001, ISO 27001 and SOC 2 Type II badges appear on /security and in the site footer, but every badge is an on-page anchor - no certificate number, auditor, audit period or report link is published, so a security team has to ask for the artefacts behind the claim
    • Annual billing is advertised as 'Save 20%' with no annual figure anywhere on the page or in its payload: the only way to learn what a year costs is to multiply the monthly price by twelve and apply the discount, which is exactly the arithmetic a buyer should not have to do alone
    • Credit consumption is a range, not a price - 5-15 credits for a quick question, 40-80 for multi-step work, 150-300 for an experimentation loop - so the plan price does not tell you what an outcome costs, and the site publishes no worked monthly example against a real customer base
    • The 'Agents at work now' counters (12.3bn signals analysed, 1.1m hypotheses written, 453,115 experiments launched, 11.3m end customers under management) carry no period, source or definition, and the company's LinkedIn page lists 2-10 employees; treat the figures as marketing until the vendor supplies the basis
    • The contract shape is enterprise - an Order Form, net-30 invoicing, VAT-exclusive fees, paid fees non-refundable except as the GTCs state, renewal terms and 45 days' notice on fee changes - which is reasonable for procurement and mismatched with the self-serve feel of the pricing cards
    • /contact returns 404: the published routes are the sales address [personal address withheld] (also the sales contact in the site's Organization JSON-LD) plus legal@, privacy@ and security@, with no support desk or help centre for a customer who has already paid

    Frequently asked questions

    What does Markin cost?

    Monthly prices in EUR plus VAT: a free tier at EUR 0 with 200 one-off welcome credits that expire after 30 days, then Pro at EUR 49/month for 750 credits, EUR 149/month for 2,500, EUR 490/month for 8,500 or EUR 990/month for 17,500. Annual billing is a 20% discount over twelve monthly payments paid once, with credits still granted monthly. Enterprise capacity is custom. The site states there is no online checkout yet, so every plan is requested and confirmed by their team before anything is charged.

    Is there a free trial?

    There is a free tier rather than a time-boxed trial: EUR 0, 200 one-off welcome credits that expire 30 calendar days after they are granted, no card and no automatic charge. It is requested through /apply rather than signed up for, and the 200 credits are a one-off allowance, not a monthly quota.

    Who operates Markin, and can I check it?

    Celerity Software, Sociedad Limitada, NIF B70839865, Carrer Eduard Maria Balcells 49, 08173 Barcelona, Spain, with Spanish law and the courts of Barcelona named in the Terms. The NIF, address and incorporation (7 March 2024, CNAE 6201) match the Registro Mercantil de Barcelona record carried by Iberinform and Cinco Días.

    Does it replace my CRM or messaging platform?

    No. Markin reads the store, CRM and messaging tools you already use and launches actions inside them; their subscriptions and sending costs stay yours and are billed separately, which the pricing FAQ says directly.

    How should I read the 38% benchmark on the homepage?

    As a vendor-run number. The chart compares Markin with several models connected through MCP, but the methodology link opens a positioning page with no sample, task set, scoring rule or dates, so there is nothing to reproduce. Ask for the underlying run before treating it as evidence.
