    Overview

    Reply Argus connects to App Store Connect and Google Play, pulls every review from both stores into one inbox, and drafts a reply in the reviewer's own language - 100+ languages, drawn from the app's own store listings, marketing page, past approved replies and any notes you add, with the cited lines shown so a draft can't invent a refund or a ship date. Nothing posts until you approve it; auto-publish is opt-in and can be scoped by rating, keyword, language or store, and on Pro and up a rules engine handles that. Alongside the inbox it clusters duplicated complaints with a match score and ranks recurring asks into a 'Signal' feed that can be exported to Notion or Sheets as a roadmap. Pricing is per reply volume rather than per seat: Indie $9.99/mo (4 apps, 300 replies), Pro $19.99/mo (10 apps, 2,000 replies, API and webhooks), Studio $99/mo (30 apps, 8,000 replies, white-label), each with a 14-day trial and a card at signup. For teams that want the queue inside an agent, /api/mcp is a remote MCP server signed in over OAuth, and /api/v1 exposes read-only apps and reviews with signed webhooks. The gaps a buyer should know about are documentary: no company, address or governing law anywhere on the site, no refund policy page, unnamed AI providers, and a signup page that advertises a free plan the pricing page denies.

    Key features

    • One inbox for App Store and Google Play reviews, with statuses of New, Drafted, Published and Skipped and per-app isolation
    • Grounded draft replies in 100+ languages, citing the store listing, marketing page, notes and past approved replies used, with contradiction flags between sources
    • Approval-first publishing: auto-publish off by default, optional for 5-star replies, or rule-scoped by rating, keyword, language or store
    • Find-similar clustering with a match score, so one reply pattern can answer a theme instead of a single review
    • Signal: recurring asks ranked by what to fix, each linked to the reviews behind it, exportable to Notion or Sheets (Jira and DevRev listed as soon)
    • Analytics with rating trend, response rate and an AI summary naming the themes behind a change
    • Remote MCP server at /api/mcp, OAuth sign-in, one-click for Cursor and Claude Desktop, CLI for Claude Code and Codex
    • Read-only REST API (/api/v1/apps, /api/v1/reviews) with keyset pagination and HMAC-signed webhooks for review.created, draft.created, reply.published, digest.weekly and anomaly.detected
    • Alerts to Slack, Telegram, Discord, email or a webhook, including rating-drop and review-spike triggers
    • White-label on Studio: your logo, product name and sending domain, with roll-up analytics across a client portfolio

    Pricing

    Official sources checked 2026-11-18

    Current official summary: No self-serve free plan is published on /pricing, which sells three monthly-or-yearly plans and Enterprise: Indie $9.99/mo or $99.90/yr ($8.33/mo billed annually) with 4 active apps and 300 replies/mo; Pro $19.99/mo or $199.90/yr ($16.66/mo) with 10 active apps and 2,000 replies, adding automation rules, auto-translate, analytics, the public REST API and signed webhooks; Studio $99/mo or $990/yr ($82.50/mo) with 30 active apps and 8,000 replies, white-label sending domain and per-app isolation; Enterprise custom. Every plan starts with a 14-day free trial and the card is required at signup: the pricing FAQ answers 'You add a card to begin, but nothing is charged until day 14'. Seats are included rather than sold (5 / 10 / 25 / unlimited) and viewers are free; the FAQ states 'You're never billed for overage'. Yearly billing is exactly ten months of the monthly price (2 months free), so 9.99x10 = 99.90, 19.99x10 = 199.90 and 99x10 = 990 all reconcile. The same three prices appear in /llms.txt, /llms-full.txt and the pricing page's JSON-LD Offer array. Two add-ons are described in the Terms (extra active-app slots billed on the regular cycle, and prepaid reply top-up packs that do not expire) but neither carries a price on /pricing. The vendor contradicts this on its own signup and login pages, which read 'Free for 1 app and 100 replies a month. You don't need a card', and the pricing FAQ answers 'Is there a free plan? No'. No purchase or trial was started; billing is by Dodo Payments as merchant of record.

    Pros

    Frequently asked questions

    What does Reply Argus cost?

    Indie is $9.99 a month or $99.90 a year for 4 active apps and 300 replies a month; Pro is $19.99 or $199.90 for 10 apps and 2,000 replies plus automation rules, the REST API and webhooks; Studio is $99 or $990 for 30 apps and 8,000 replies with white-label and per-app isolation; Enterprise is custom. Every plan carries a 14-day trial with a card at signup, billed from day 14, and the yearly price is exactly ten monthly payments. Two caveats: the signup and login pages advertise a card-free free plan ('Free for 1 app and 100 replies a month') that /pricing answers with 'Is there a free plan? No', and the extra-app-slot and reply-top-up add-ons described in the Terms carry no published price.

    Does it post replies without approval?

    No, not unless you turn that on. Approval mode is the default and auto-publish is opt-in, limited to 5-star replies if you want the narrowest rule, or scoped by rating, keyword, language or store on Pro and up. That default matters because both stores allow exactly one public reply per review and Google Play exposes no delete method - the vendor's own reference pages document both constraints.

    Which stores does it cover, and what does it need from them?

    Apple's App Store and Google Play only - no Microsoft Store, Steam or web stores. You connect App Store Connect and the Play Console once and it polls both, because neither store pushes a webhook for a new review. The vendor's API references put the practical limits at roughly 350 characters per Play reply and the 7-day reply window on both platforms.

    Who is behind it, and where is the company?

    Nothing on the site says. There is no company name, no registered address, no company number and no governing-law clause in the Terms, and the only contact route published is support@replyargus.com (the footer 'Contact' link is a mailto and /contact returns 404). The domain was registered on 2 July 2026, the product launched on Product Hunt on 28 July 2026, and the blog is bylined 'The Argus Team'. Ask before paying annually.

    Can an AI agent drive it?

    Yes - that is a first-class feature rather than an add-on. /api/mcp is a remote MCP server that signs in over OAuth (not an API key) and is scoped to your workspace, with publish calls gated on approval; the site publishes one-click setup for Cursor and Claude Desktop and CLI/TOML snippets for Claude Code and Codex. Separately, Pro and up get a read-only REST API and HMAC-signed webhooks for your own tooling.
