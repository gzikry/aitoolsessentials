The $130 coding stack

The problem isn’t that AI tools are expensive; it’s that they make the same promise in different interfaces.

Where noted, prices are verified from official pricing pages checked 2026-08-25 (and Grok/API token rates). Still verify before you pay.

The overlap problem

Paying for three coding assistants is often just three invoices for one job. On 2026-08-25, Copilot Pro is $10/month, Cursor Pro is $20/month, and Claude Max starts at $100/month—aka a $130/month stack for the same bugfix workflow.

Cursor’s official pricing (checked 2026-08-25) lists Hobby as free, Pro at $20, Pro+ at $60, Ultra at $200, and Teams at $40/user/month; on-demand usage is billed in arrears. So the “what am I actually buying?” question matters more than the headline tier name.

Pick one paid surface per job

Verdict: don’t hunt for one universal winner. Choose one paid surface per job, then cancel everything else that runs the same job through a different UI.

If your work is an AI-native editor—multi-file changes, refactors, and iterative edits—keep Cursor. That’s where the “edit the repo until it works” loop lives.

If you mostly want inline help while you work in an editor you already trust, Copilot is the cleaner layer. It’s the completion lane, not a second IDE you also pay to learn.

Claude Code/Max is for a specific kind of work: terminal-agent time on a repo. If that’s not the job you’re doing, paying for Claude “because it’s smart” just adds overlap.

  • Inline completions in your existing editor → Copilot
  • AI-native IDE workflows (multi-file edits) → Cursor
  • Repo-agent work (terminal orchestration) → Claude Code/Max

And don’t conflate the Claude invoices: Claude Pro is $20/month-to-month or $200/year, while Claude Max starts at $100/month. Pro isn’t Max; Max isn’t “everything Claude.”

Quick sanity-checks before you renew

Grok moves fast, so verify before buying. The official snapshot checked 2026-08-25 lists SuperGrok at $30/month and SuperGrok Plus at $100/month. For API callers, Grok 4.6 is $2 input / $6 output per 1M tokens—those token rates can turn “a cheap seat” into a real cost center.

Same rule for the one-off subscriptions: HtmlSlides Pro has a $9.90/month launch offer, and Whop CLI is free to install but adds platform transaction fees if you’re selling. Treat them as “job tools,” not as an extra chat window that duplicates your editor.

ChatGPT is the one place I’d pause: the GPT-5.4 family was recorded 2026-08-26, but no reliable US-dollar Plus/Pro amount was exposed on the official pricing page—so don’t guess a number. Verify before you lock in an annual.
