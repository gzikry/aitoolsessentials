    Development · Free: USD 0, with no tiers, no paid plan, no seat count and no account for the local app. Licensed FSL-1.1-MIT (copyright 2026 Amir Fish), which is source-available rather than OSI open source: you may use, copy, modify and redistribute it for any Permitted Purpose, a Competing Use is barred, and each version converts to the MIT license on its second anniversary.     CCC (Claude Command Center)

    CCC (Claude Command Center) is a local dashboard for people running several coding agents at once. Instead of owning execution, it reads each engine's on-disk state as the source of truth, so a session you started in a terminal, resumed by hand or launched headless outside the dashboard still shows up on the board - that is the difference between a dashboard and a second place you have to remember to work from. On top of the board it adds a needs-attention lane that flags sessions ending on a real question, a per-session context meter, plan-usage and rate-limit projections, a Kanban with a project tree and an infinite Flow canvas, group chats between agents, a work queue with verify-to-close receipts, git worktrees per task, and an optional fallback that keeps a limit-hit session running on a $0 model. The stack is deliberately plain: a stdlib-only Python server bound to 127.0.0.1 and a no-build vanilla-JS UI, with readable JSON state under ~/.claude/command-center/. Claude Code is first-class; Codex, Cursor, Antigravity, Kilo Code, Kimi Code, OpenCode and Devin spawn and ingest with documented gaps. Version 5.39.0 shipped 2026-10-18 and the repository was pushed the same day this review was written.

    Best for: Developers who already run several coding agents and keep losing track of them in terminal tabs. CCC is worth a look if you launch Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and friends from a terminal and want one local board that shows every session, which one is waiting on you, and which has quietly run out of context - without routing your work through someone else's cloud. It is not a fit for a single-session user (a terminal is enough), for anyone who wants a hosted team product with accounts and permissions, for a Windows or Linux shop expecting parity with the macOS app, or for a buyer who needs a company entity and a governing-law clause before installing software.

    AI Agents · Monthly subscriptions in EUR plus VAT, sold through a request form rather than a checkout, with a free tier. Free: EUR 0 for 200 one-off welcome credits that expire 30 calendar days after they are granted, no card and no automatic charge, but access is requested rather than signed up. Pro at four capacity tiers: EUR 49/month for 750 credits, EUR 149/month for 2,500, EUR 490/month for 8,500 and EUR 990/month for 17,500, credits shared by the whole team with no per-seat fee. Annual billing is a 20% discount over twelve monthly payments, paid once for the year, with the credits still granted monthly rather than all at once; no annual figure is published anywhere on the page, so the discounted total has to be computed from the monthly price. Unused credits roll over into one additional monthly period and expire at the end of it, oldest credits consumed first; cancelling a monthly plan runs to the end of the paid month, and cancelling an annual plan stops renewal while access and the monthly credit grants continue to the end of the paid year. Enterprise is custom - agreed capacity, tailored integrations, a forward deployed engineer, DPA and security review, adapted invoicing. The pricing FAQ states 'there is no online checkout yet' and that the team confirms access, configuration and billing before anything is charged. No purchase was attempted.

    Markin

    Markin is an agentic growth platform for large direct-to-consumer businesses. You connect a store, CRM and warehouse; its agents read customer behaviour, propose revenue opportunities with a size attached, prepare or launch the intervention in the tools you already run, and read each result against a control group. The pitch is deliberately narrow - incremental ARPU, not more dashboards - and the vendor is unusually candid on its own comparison page that an LLM wired to your data through MCP can do parts of this work; what it sells is the assembled system, the experiment bookkeeping, the audit trail and the team that stands behind it. Access is sales-led: the free allowance and every paid plan begin with a request form, and the pricing FAQ says outright that there is no online checkout yet.

    Best for: D2C growth, CRM or lifecycle teams sitting on a large customer base and an existing stack (Shopify, Klaviyo, Braze, Salesforce, a warehouse) who want an agent system to find revenue opportunities, size them and run holdout-measured experiments - rather than another dashboard or another set of segments to build by hand

    Audio & Video · One-time or monthly subscription, USD. $19.90 per song pack (one-time, no subscription) or $9.90/month for three song packs a month, renewing monthly. One song pack = two generated song versions plus one included regenerate, MP3 downloads and a shareable gift page. A lyric draft and a 30-second audio preview of both versions are free before payment, subject to rolling 24-hour limits and daily capacity. The same $19.90 and $9.90 figures appear on /pricing and on the /zh pricing page. Refunds are a single Terms clause rather than a policy page ('payments are not automatically refundable after generation work has started or a paid credit has been used'). No purchase was attempted.

    MeloLetter

    MeloLetter turns a written story - names, memories, the things you meant to say - into a personalised song gift. You answer guided prompts, get an AI lyric draft you can edit before paying, hear a free 30-second preview of both generated versions, and then buy a song pack to unlock full playback, MP3 downloads and a public shareable gift page. Songs come from Suno v5.5 through the YourMusic API and lyrics from GPT-5.5 through Brioi, both named with model identifiers in the terms. It sells one pack at $19.90 or three a month at $9.90. What stands out for a four-month-old site is documentation: the privacy policy names its analytics stack with retention periods, the terms carry a usable acceptable-use policy with a moderation and appeals process, and the examples page labels its demos 'illustrative' instead of dressing them as customer stories. What is missing is who runs it - no company, individual, postal address or governing law - and there is no refund page, only that clause.

    Best for: People buying a one-off keepsake gift - a birthday, an anniversary, a proposal, a thank-you to a parent or friend - who want to hear and read the thing before they pay for it, and who would rather spend $19.90 once than hold a music-generation subscription they will use twice a year
