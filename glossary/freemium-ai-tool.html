    LayerGrab

    LayerGrab splits a flat image into editable, named layers with AI: upload or right-click an image, get each element (text, products, graphics) on its own transparent layer with the background filled in behind them, and export PNG, ZIP or a layered PSD. It works in the browser, a Chrome extension (listed on the Chrome Web Store), and - per its privacy page - Photoshop, Figma and Sketch plugins. The credit math is spelled out with worked examples on the pricing page (a 10-layer poster at 10 credits per layer on Basic costs $1.10). The gaps are identity and consumer paperwork: no company or jurisdiction is named anywhere, there is no refund policy page and no contact page, and the AI model doing the splitting is described only as 'our processing partner'.

    Best for: Designers, ecommerce sellers and social-media editors who need to pull one element off a flat image - a product, a poster's text, a sticker - as a clean transparent layer without opening desktop software. The Chrome extension (right-click any image on the web) is the fastest version of the job. Not a fit for anyone who needs a named company and a refund policy before paying, or for bulk API automation (no developer API is offered).

    Faceloria

    Faceloria runs an AI face analysis in the browser: you upload a front-facing photo and get a face-shape category, symmetry and proportion measurements, plus an optional one-time Full Report with a styling action plan. The pitch leans on a 0-100 'attractiveness score' alongside the geometry, and the site itself frames results as informational. The operation is unusually transparent about the data path for a face tool: payments are handled by Waffo, source photos sit in Cloudflare R2 and are automatically deleted within 48 hours, and a complete legal set (privacy, terms, cookie and acceptable-use pages) is in place with a recent update date. The weak spots are identity and analytics: no company, controller or governing law is named anywhere, and the pages load a Plausible-compatible analytics script from pvuv.ai while the privacy policy says only that analytics 'run only when enabled by deployment configuration'.

    Best for: People curious about face-shape analysis who want a structured facial-measurement report rather than a horoscope-style app: upload one front-facing photo, get a face-shape category, width-to-height ratio and vertical-balance measurements for free, then decide whether the $16.99 one-time Full Report (4-week styling action plan and PDF) is worth it. Not a fit for anyone who needs a named company, jurisdiction or refund policy before paying, or for anyone who finds a 0-100 attractiveness score unhelpful - that framing is part of the product.
