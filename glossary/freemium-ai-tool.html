    AI tools glossary

    Freemium AI tool

    A tool with a free tier plus paid upgrades for higher limits, team features, exports, integrations, or commercial usage.

    Practical evaluation note

    Test the free tier, but check upgrade pressure before building business workflows around it.

    LayerGrab

    LayerGrab splits a flat image into editable, named layers with AI: upload or right-click an image, get each element (text, products, graphics) on its own transparent layer with the background filled in behind them, and export PNG, ZIP or a layered PSD. It works in the browser, a Chrome extension (listed on the Chrome Web Store), and - per its privacy page - Photoshop, Figma and Sketch plugins. The credit math is spelled out with worked examples on the pricing page (a 10-layer poster at 10 credits per layer on Basic costs $1.10). The gaps are identity and consumer paperwork: no company or jurisdiction is named anywhere, there is no refund policy page and no contact page, and the AI model doing the splitting is described only as 'our processing partner'.

    Best for: Designers, ecommerce sellers and social-media editors who need to pull one element off a flat image - a product, a poster's text, a sticker - as a clean transparent layer without opening desktop software. The Chrome extension (right-click any image on the web) is the fastest version of the job. Not a fit for anyone who needs a named company and a refund policy before paying, or for bulk API automation (no developer API is offered).

    Development · No self-serve free plan is published on /pricing, which sells three monthly-or-yearly plans and Enterprise: Indie $9.99/mo or $99.90/yr ($8.33/mo billed annually) with 4 active apps and 300 replies/mo; Pro $19.99/mo or $199.90/yr ($16.66/mo) with 10 active apps and 2,000 replies, adding automation rules, auto-translate, analytics, the public REST API and signed webhooks; Studio $99/mo or $990/yr ($82.50/mo) with 30 active apps and 8,000 replies, white-label sending domain and per-app isolation; Enterprise custom. Every plan starts with a 14-day free trial and the card is required at signup: the pricing FAQ answers 'You add a card to begin, but nothing is charged until day 14'. Seats are included rather than sold (5 / 10 / 25 / unlimited) and viewers are free; the FAQ states 'You're never billed for overage'. Yearly billing is exactly ten months of the monthly price (2 months free), so 9.99x10 = 99.90, 19.99x10 = 199.90 and 99x10 = 990 all reconcile. The same three prices appear in /llms.txt, /llms-full.txt and the pricing page's JSON-LD Offer array. Two add-ons are described in the Terms (extra active-app slots billed on the regular cycle, and prepaid reply top-up packs that do not expire) but neither carries a price on /pricing. The vendor contradicts this on its own signup and login pages, which read 'Free for 1 app and 100 replies a month. You don't need a card', and the pricing FAQ answers 'Is there a free plan? No'. No purchase or trial was started; billing is by Dodo Payments as merchant of record.

    Reply Argus

    Reply Argus connects to App Store Connect and Google Play, pulls every review from both stores into one inbox, and drafts a reply in the reviewer's own language - 100+ languages, drawn from the app's own store listings, marketing page, past approved replies and any notes you add, with the cited lines shown so a draft can't invent a refund or a ship date. Nothing posts until you approve it; auto-publish is opt-in and can be scoped by rating, keyword, language or store, and on Pro and up a rules engine handles that. Alongside the inbox it clusters duplicated complaints with a match score and ranks recurring asks into a 'Signal' feed that can be exported to Notion or Sheets as a roadmap. Pricing is per reply volume rather than per seat: Indie $9.99/mo (4 apps, 300 replies), Pro $19.99/mo (10 apps, 2,000 replies, API and webhooks), Studio $99/mo (30 apps, 8,000 replies, white-label), each with a 14-day trial and a card at signup. For teams that want the queue inside an agent, /api/mcp is a remote MCP server signed in over OAuth, and /api/v1 exposes read-only apps and reviews with signed webhooks. The gaps a buyer should know about are documentary: no company, address or governing law anywhere on the site, no refund policy page, unnamed AI providers, and a signup page that advertises a free plan the pricing page denies.

    Best for: iOS and Android developers, app studios and agencies who would rather answer App Store and Google Play reviews in the reviewer's own language than paste the same English template at every 1-star - especially teams that want an approval gate before anything posts to a store, and developers who want the review queue reachable from an AI agent over MCP or from their own read-only REST integration

    AI Image to Prompt

    AI Image to Prompt is a free browser tool that reads an uploaded image and writes the text prompt that would recreate something like it, formatted for the image model you name. You supply a JPG, PNG or WebP up to 8MB by drag-and-drop, file picker, clipboard paste or public image URL, pick an output style (General, Nano Banana, Flux, Midjourney with comma-separated descriptors and parameters, or Stable Diffusion with tags and a suggested negative prompt), and optionally an output language from eight options, then copy an editable result. A text-to-prompt mode handles the same job without an image, and a prompt history is kept in the browser. No account is required and the site says uploads are deleted after processing, with generation run through kie.ai on Cloudflare infrastructure. It describes visible elements - subject, setting, composition, perspective, lighting, palette, style, mood - and the site is explicit that it does not recover the original hidden prompt behind an AI image, only a reproduction-oriented approximation. Verification was read-only: the /api/generate route is live and rejects an empty body with 400 {"error":"no_image"}, but no generation was run, so output quality is not assessed here.

    Best for: Creators and small teams who already have a reference image and need a written brief for it: illustrators testing an art direction, designers producing concept variants, marketers fixing a campaign's visual language from a moodboard, and hobbyists learning how image prompts are structured. It fits the free-and-anonymous case well - no account, eight output languages, model-specific formatting for Midjourney, Stable Diffusion, Flux and Nano Banana - and it fits someone who wants one editable paragraph rather than four variants at once. It is a poor fit if you need to know who operates the site, what law governs it, or what anything costs: there is no company, controller, postal address or named jurisdiction anywhere, the only address published is a personal Gmail, the pricing page belongs to a different product, and six five-star testimonials from named people carry no evidence they are real.

    Creative · One-time credit packs, USD, no subscription: Starter $14.90 for 400 credits (40 text edits, $0.37 per edit), Pro $26.90 for 1,000 credits (100 edits, $0.27), Studio $39.90 for 1,800 credits (180 edits, $0.22).     Change Text in Image

    Change Text in Image does one narrow job and does it in a browser: you upload a JPG, PNG or static WebP up to 10MB / 20MP, drag a box over the words you want to replace, type the original text and the replacement, and the model redraws the wording in the font, colour and style it detected while rebuilding the background behind the old letters. Leaving the replacement empty removes the text instead. There are no font, size or colour controls, by design - the promise is an edit that looks like it was always there - and the neighbouring pages cover the same workflow for screenshots and translations, plus a blur tool that runs entirely on your device. Pricing is one-time credit packs only: $14.90 for 40 edits, $26.90 for 100 and $39.90 for 180, with 10 free credits at sign-up. The paperwork is unusually straight for this class - the operators are named as an individual with a working contact, Cloudflare, fal.ai's Nano Banana and Waffo are named by company and function, prompt screening before generation is disclosed, and the policy refuses to claim zero retention or supplier-wide no-training. What holds it back is that it says tracking is not active while Google Analytics, Microsoft Clarity and a third-party visitor counter all load on the policy page itself, and that no company, address or jurisdiction appears anywhere.

    Best for: Creators, e-commerce sellers and small marketing teams who need to change words already baked into a finished image - a typo on an exported ad, a price on a product shot, a date on an invitation, a label in a screenshot - without the source file, priced per edit at 10 credits with a one-time pack instead of a subscription. Also worth a look if you want a text-blur tool that provably runs on your own device. Not a fit if you need a named company and jurisdiction before paying, if you need account password recovery, if you need a committed deletion deadline for uploaded images, or if you are editing IDs, receipts, bank statements or other evidence (banned in its own acceptable-use policy).
