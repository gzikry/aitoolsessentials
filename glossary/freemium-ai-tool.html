    Freemium AI tool

    A tool with a free tier plus paid upgrades for higher limits, team features, exports, integrations, or commercial usage.

    LayerGrab

    LayerGrab splits a flat image into editable, named layers with AI: upload or right-click an image, get each element (text, products, graphics) on its own transparent layer with the background filled in behind them, and export PNG, ZIP or a layered PSD. It works in the browser, a Chrome extension (listed on the Chrome Web Store), and - per its privacy page - Photoshop, Figma and Sketch plugins. The credit math is spelled out with worked examples on the pricing page (a 10-layer poster at 10 credits per layer on Basic costs $1.10). The gaps are identity and consumer paperwork: no company or jurisdiction is named anywhere, there is no refund policy page and no contact page, and the AI model doing the splitting is described only as 'our processing partner'.

    Best for: Designers, ecommerce sellers and social-media editors who need to pull one element off a flat image - a product, a poster's text, a sticker - as a clean transparent layer without opening desktop software. The Chrome extension (right-click any image on the web) is the fastest version of the job. Not a fit for anyone who needs a named company and a refund policy before paying, or for bulk API automation (no developer API is offered).

    AI Image to Prompt

    AI Image to Prompt is a free browser tool that reads an uploaded image and writes the text prompt that would recreate something like it, formatted for the image model you name. You supply a JPG, PNG or WebP up to 8MB by drag-and-drop, file picker, clipboard paste or public image URL, pick an output style (General, Nano Banana, Flux, Midjourney with comma-separated descriptors and parameters, or Stable Diffusion with tags and a suggested negative prompt), and optionally an output language from eight options, then copy an editable result. A text-to-prompt mode handles the same job without an image, and a prompt history is kept in the browser. No account is required and the site says uploads are deleted after processing, with generation run through kie.ai on Cloudflare infrastructure. It describes visible elements - subject, setting, composition, perspective, lighting, palette, style, mood - and the site is explicit that it does not recover the original hidden prompt behind an AI image, only a reproduction-oriented approximation. Verification was read-only: the /api/generate route is live and rejects an empty body with 400 {"error":"no_image"}, but no generation was run, so output quality is not assessed here.

    Best for: Creators and small teams who already have a reference image and need a written brief for it: illustrators testing an art direction, designers producing concept variants, marketers fixing a campaign's visual language from a moodboard, and hobbyists learning how image prompts are structured. It fits the free-and-anonymous case well - no account, eight output languages, model-specific formatting for Midjourney, Stable Diffusion, Flux and Nano Banana - and it fits someone who wants one editable paragraph rather than four variants at once. It is a poor fit if you need to know who operates the site, what law governs it, or what anything costs: there is no company, controller, postal address or named jurisdiction anywhere, the only address published is a personal Gmail, the pricing page belongs to a different product, and six five-star testimonials from named people carry no evidence they are real.

    DREAMMMER

    DREAMMMER is a browser AI image and video editor built around an agent rather than a model picker: you describe the result, it writes the English prompt, chooses among GPT Image 2.5, Nano Banana 2.1, Seedream 5.0, Qwen Image 3.0, Grok Imagine, Kling 3.0, Veo 3.1, Seedance 2.5 and MiniMax H3, and you refine in the same thread while every step is mapped on a canvas. Eleven one-click tools (background remove, upscale, object remover, photo restore, clothes and background changer, expand) sit in front of the same credit meter, and a public showcase publishes real prompts with the model and credit cost behind each result. The commercial terms are unusually clear for a four-month-old product: you own what you create, the site states it does not train its own models on your uploads, failed generations refund credits automatically, and there is a written 7-day refund window with a 20% usage cap. What is missing is identity and data detail rather than honesty: no registered company is named anywhere, there is no governing-law clause, the policy promises to say how long data is kept and never gives a period, and Google Analytics loads on the privacy policy itself while the policy names no analytics vendor.

    Best for: Marketers, designers and small teams who want one subscription that reaches GPT Image, Nano Banana, Seedream, Qwen, Grok, Kling, Veo, Seedance and MiniMax models without buying each one - an agent writes the prompt and picks the model, 50 free sign-up credits, then $29-$299 a month or a $999 one-time pack. Not a fit if you need a named company or a governing-law clause before you pay, if you expect work made on the free tier to stay private (it can be published in the showcase with its prompts and reference images), or if you need subscription credits to roll over month to month.

    Faceloria

    Faceloria runs an AI face analysis in the browser: you upload a front-facing photo and get a face-shape category, symmetry and proportion measurements, plus an optional one-time Full Report with a styling action plan. The pitch leans on a 0-100 'attractiveness score' alongside the geometry, and the site itself frames results as informational. The operation is unusually transparent about the data path for a face tool: payments are handled by Waffo, source photos sit in Cloudflare R2 and are automatically deleted within 48 hours, and a complete legal set (privacy, terms, cookie and acceptable-use pages) is in place with a recent update date. The weak spots are identity and analytics: no company, controller or governing law is named anywhere, and the pages load a Plausible-compatible analytics script from pvuv.ai while the privacy policy says only that analytics 'run only when enabled by deployment configuration'.

    Best for: People curious about face-shape analysis who want a structured facial-measurement report rather than a horoscope-style app: upload one front-facing photo, get a face-shape category, width-to-height ratio and vertical-balance measurements for free, then decide whether the $16.99 one-time Full Report (4-week styling action plan and PDF) is worth it. Not a fit for anyone who needs a named company, jurisdiction or refund policy before paying, or for anyone who finds a 0-100 attractiveness score unhelpful - that framing is part of the product.
