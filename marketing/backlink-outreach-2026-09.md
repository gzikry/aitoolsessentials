## Delivery-failure handling notes

- **Corrected sweep v2 (2026-09-11, authoritative).** A `[Gmail]/Sent Mail`-only sweep undercounts, because Gmail files some outbound messages in `[Gmail]/Trash`. Scanning Sent + Trash and deduplicating by (date, subject, recipient) gives the real September picture:
  - **September 2026 outbound: 78 sends across 68 distinct recipient domains.**
  - **Sept 9 batch alone: 61 sends across 58 distinct domains** (Sent Mail contributed 59/56; Trash held 2 more — `contact@aigearbase.com` and `hello@llmversus.com`).
  - **Three September domains have no MX at all: `aitoolswise.com`, `aigearbase.com`, `llmversus.com`.** All three are dead ends.
  - This supersedes both earlier figures ("exactly one no-MX domain" and then "52 sends / two no-MX domains"). Both were wrong for the same root cause: the sweep source was wrong, not just incomplete. A curated target list misses recipients added outside it; Sent-Mail-only misses sends Gmail filed to Trash.
- Lesson: MX-check the **exact domain from the `To:` header of every send**, drawn from **every folder that can hold outbound mail** — not a curated target list and not Sent Mail alone.
- Two hard bounces on Sept 11 were for addresses the vendors themselves publish (`support@videoupscaler.video`) or that the outreach list assumed (`info@stackaible.com`). Neither is worth a retry without a working address from the vendor.
- Bounce subjects arrive as `Delivery Status Notification (Delay)` first and `(Failure)` later; a Delay is not actionable on its own — check the referenced `To:` header inside the attached `message/rfc822` block, not the `mailer-daemon` envelope.
- **A Delay is not evidence of a dead end by itself, and a live website is not evidence of a working mailbox.** `aigearbase.com` and `llmversus.com` both return HTTP 200 with polished content and publish a contact address; both domains have no MX at all. `aigearbase.com` also has a non-functional contact form (`alert()` + `reset()`, no fetch, `/api/contact` and `/contact.php` → 404). Always resolve MX before treating a published contact as reachable.
- **Correction (2026-09-12): "no MX means no bounce will ever arrive" was wrong.** Per RFC 5321 a domain with no MX falls back to its A record as an implicit MX, so MTAs still attempt delivery and *do* eventually emit a final DSN — Gmail just took three days of retries to get there. `llmversus.com` produced no DSN on Sept 11 (declared a "silent dead end, invisible to bounce-driven detection") but delivered `Action: failed` / `4.4.1` on Sept 12, filed to `[Gmail]/Spam`; `aitoolswise.com` likewise failed finally at the same time. The practical rule is unchanged (MX-check before sending, because a no-MX domain fails *silently for days* and is invisible to a monitor that only sees same-day bounces) — but a no-MX domain is **slow-detection**, not undetectable. Do not assert "no bounce will ever arrive"; expect the final DSN up to ~3 days later, and check `[Gmail]/Spam` as well as INBOX for it.
