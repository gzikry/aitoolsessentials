How to test before paying

  1. Start with de-identified administrative material: staff meeting notes, SOP drafts, scheduling handoffs, or training outlines.
  2. Do not paste patient identifiers, protected health information, clinical images, lab data, or diagnosis-specific details into a general AI tool unless your organization has approved the vendor, contracts, retention settings, and compliance controls.
  3. Separate clinical judgment from administrative drafting: AI can help prepare summaries and checklists, but licensed professionals must make medical, billing, legal, and compliance decisions.
  4. Before recording visits or staff meetings, verify consent rules, retention settings, export controls, and whether a compliant agreement is available from the vendor.

Safety and compliance guardrails

  • Not medical advice: these recommendations cover administrative and research-support workflows, not diagnosis, treatment, triage, prescribing, or patient-specific clinical decisions.
  • No HIPAA claim is implied: verify each vendor's healthcare terms, business associate agreement availability, data retention, audit logs, admin controls, and your organization's policies before using patient data.
  • Prefer de-identified test cases during tool trials; keep final patient-facing, billing, and compliance language under qualified human review.
